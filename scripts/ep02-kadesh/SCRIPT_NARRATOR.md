# Kadesh: The First Fake News in History
## Full Script — Narrator Only (~10 minutes)

**Language:** English
**Voice:** One narrator (same tone as episode 1: dramatic, dry humour, fast-paced)
**Characters:** Silent, animate only

---

## 0. COLD OPEN (0:00–0:40)

[*Black. Desert wind. Fade in: a golden chariot racing across a dusty plain, a walled city on a river behind it.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"1274 BC. Syria. The most powerful man in the world is about to be ambushed."

[*Ramesses II in his chariot, chin up, smug.*]

"His name is Ramesses the Second. Pharaoh of Egypt. Son of the sun god. Twenty-five years old. And very, very sure of himself."

"In a few hours, his army will be cut in half, his camp will be overrun, and he will be fighting for his life."

[*Hittite chariots burst out from behind the city walls.*]

"And yet, when it was all over, he went home and carved his great victory into the walls of five temples."

[*Ramesses' face, carved in stone, smiling.*]

"Meanwhile, the other king went home... and recorded his victory too."

"So who actually won the Battle of Kadesh?"

[*TITLE CARD: KADESH*]

---

## 1. TWO SUPERPOWERS (0:40–2:00)

[*Narrator text card: "CORRECTION".*]

**NARRATOR:**
"Quick correction first. Last episode, we said this was a battle between two pharaohs. It wasn't. Only one of them was a pharaoh. The other was a Hittite king. Which honestly makes the story even better."

[*Map of the Middle East. Egypt in gold, the Hittite empire in blue.*]

"Around 1300 BC, the Middle East had two superpowers. In the south, Egypt. Rich, ancient, and ruled by god-kings for well over a thousand years. In the north, the Hittites. An empire from the mountains of what is now Turkey, with tough soldiers and some of the best chariots in the world."

[*The strip between them flashes: Syria and Canaan.*]

"And between them sat a strip of land everyone wanted. Syria and Canaan. Trade routes, cities, and gold. Whoever held it ruled the region."

[*Zoom to a walled city on a river.*]

"The key to it all was a fortified city on the Orontes River. Its name was Kadesh."

---

## 2. THE YOUNG PHARAOH (2:00–3:00)

[*Ramesses on his throne, pointing at a map.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"Ramesses wanted Kadesh. His father had captured it once, and lost it again. So in the fifth year of his reign, Ramesses marched north with one of the biggest armies Egypt had ever assembled. Around twenty thousand men."

[*Four columns of Egyptian soldiers, each with a different banner.*]

"Four divisions, each named after a god. Amun. Ra. Ptah. And Set."

[*Side by side: a light two-man Egyptian chariot and a heavier three-man Hittite chariot.*]

"And the weapon of the age was the chariot. Egyptian chariots were light and fast. Two men: a driver and an archer, shooting from a moving platform. Hittite chariots were heavier. Three men: a driver, a shield bearer, and a spearman. Built to smash straight into the enemy. Think sports car against tank."

[*Hittite king on a throne in a dark mountain hall. Calm. Stroking his beard.*]

"Waiting for him was the Hittite king, Muwatalli the Second. Older. Quieter. And much, much sneakier."

---

## 3. THE TWO SPIES (3:00–4:15)

[*Two scruffy nomads bowing before Ramesses.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"Near Kadesh, the Egyptians met two local nomads. They said they wanted to switch sides. And they had great news. The Hittite army was far away, up north near Aleppo. Too scared to face the mighty pharaoh."

[*Ramesses grinning, thumbs up.*]

"Ramesses loved this. He didn't check. He took his first division, Amun, and raced ahead to build his camp right next to Kadesh. The rest of his army was strung out for miles behind him."

[*Map: one Egyptian column near the city, three far behind.*]

"There was just one problem. The two nomads were Hittite spies."

[*The nomads wink at each other.*]

"The entire Hittite army was hiding on the other side of the city. Thousands of chariots. Close enough to see the Egyptian campfires."

---

## 4. THE TRUTH COMES OUT (4:15–5:00)

[*Two Hittite scouts tied up, sweating, in front of Egyptian soldiers.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"Then Egyptian patrols caught two real Hittite scouts. After a beating, they told the truth. The Hittites weren't in Aleppo. They were right there. Behind Kadesh. Ready."

[*Ramesses shouting at his officers. Messengers galloping away.*]

"Ramesses was furious. He yelled at his officers and sent messengers racing south to hurry the rest of the army."

"It was too late."

---

## 5. THE CHARIOTS STRIKE (5:00–6:15)

[*Overhead map: Hittite chariots cross the river and hit the marching Ra division from the side.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"The Hittite chariots crossed the river and slammed into the second division, Ra, while it was still marching in a long, loose column. Thousands of chariots. Each one carrying three men. The Ra division didn't form up. It didn't fight. It just broke."

[*Egyptian soldiers running, dropping shields.*]

"The survivors ran north, straight into Ramesses' camp. With the Hittite chariots right behind them."

[*Chaos in the camp. Tents collapsing. Ramesses alone in the middle.*]

"Now the camp was in chaos. Soldiers fleeing. Tents trampled. And the Pharaoh of Egypt was nearly surrounded."

---

## 6. RAMESSES FIGHTS BACK (6:15–7:45)

[*Ramesses, glowing, alone in his chariot, firing arrows at a sea of enemies.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"Here's where the story gets interesting. According to Ramesses himself, everyone abandoned him. So he prayed to the god Amun, climbed into his chariot, and charged the enemy. Alone. Again and again. Defeating thousands with his own hands."

[*Text card: "HIS VERSION".*]

"That's his version. The temple carvings even show his pet lion in the camp. Historians are a little skeptical."

[*Realistic version: Ramesses with a small bodyguard, fighting hard.*]

"What probably happened is this. Ramesses really did fight bravely with his bodyguard. And the Hittite charioteers made a classic mistake. They stopped to loot the Egyptian camp. Gold. Food. Weapons."

[*Hittite soldiers grabbing treasure. A new Egyptian unit appears on the horizon.*]

"And then a fresh Egyptian unit arrived from the coast. The Ne'arin. They hit the distracted Hittites from the side. Then the Ptah division finally showed up."

[*Hittite chariots pulling back across the river.*]

"Muwatalli sent in another thousand chariots. But his huge army of foot soldiers never crossed the river. By nightfall, the Hittite chariots had pulled back."

---

## 7. WHO WON? (7:45–9:00)

[*Two armies facing each other across the river at dawn.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"The next day, the two armies fought again. Nobody broke through. Ramesses couldn't take Kadesh, and his army was tired and far from home."

[*Ramesses' chariot turning around, heading south.*]

"So he turned around and marched back to Egypt."

[*Map: Kadesh stays blue. Blue spreads a little further south.*]

"Kadesh stayed Hittite. The Hittites even pushed further south afterwards. On paper, that looks a lot like a Hittite win."

[*Giant temple walls covered in carvings of Ramesses crushing enemies.*]

"But back home, Ramesses had his version carved into the walls of his greatest temples. Karnak. Luxor. Abydos. The Ramesseum. Abu Simbel. Huge scenes of him alone in his chariot, crushing the Hittites. It might be the first big piece of military propaganda in history."

[*Hittite clay tablets in the mountain capital.*]

"And in the Hittite records, the story was very different. The Egyptian king had been beaten, and sent home."

[*Split screen: Ramesses smug on the left, Muwatalli smug on the right.*]

"For a long time, nobody could check. Historians had almost forgotten the Hittites existed. Then, a little over a hundred years ago, archaeologists dug up the Hittite capital, Hattusa, and found thousands of clay tablets. Finally, we could hear the other side."

"Two kings. One battle. Two completely different stories."

---

## 8. THE PEACE TREATY (9:00–9:50)

[*Two kings' messengers exchanging a clay tablet and a silver tablet.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"But here's the twist. About sixteen years later, Egypt and the Hittites stopped fighting and signed a peace treaty. One of the oldest peace treaties ever found. They promised not to attack each other, to help each other against enemies, and to send back each other's runaways."

[*Wedding scene: Ramesses and a Hittite princess.*]

"The Egyptian copy was carved in hieroglyphs on temple walls. The Hittite copy was written on clay tablets in Hattusa. Archaeologists found both, and they match almost line by line. Except for one detail. Each side says the other side asked for peace first."

"Ramesses even married a Hittite princess."

[*Modern building with flags. A clay tablet on display.*]

"Today, a copy of that treaty is on display at the United Nations in New York. The oldest peace deal, in the home of modern peace deals."

---

## 9. OUTRO (9:50–10:30)

[*Empty plain by the river. Wind. Grass.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"So who won the Battle of Kadesh? On the battlefield, probably nobody. Ramesses survived a trap that should have destroyed him. Muwatalli kept Kadesh. And in the end, both of them decided the war wasn't worth it."

[*Giant stone statue of Ramesses at Abu Simbel.*]

"But Ramesses won something else. More than three thousand years later, we still know his name, and we still read his version. He didn't just fight the battle. He won the story."

[*Title card: Next episode — Assyria*]

"Next time: Assyria. The most feared empire on Earth, and how it disappeared in just a few years. Subscribe to Spears and Tears, so you don't miss it."

## PRODUCTION NOTES

**Characters needed:** Ramesses II (young pharaoh, blue-and-gold crown, smug), Muwatalli II (Hittite king, beard, tall hat), two Shasu nomads (spies), two Hittite scouts, Egyptian soldiers (four division banners), Hittite chariots (three men each), Ne'arin troops, a Hittite princess.

**Sources:** Ramesses' "Poem" and "Bulletin" inscriptions (Karnak, Luxor, Abydos, Ramesseum, Abu Simbel); Hittite correspondence from Hattusa; the Egyptian–Hittite peace treaty (c. 1259 BC). Mark Healy, *Qadesh 1300 BC*; Toby Wilkinson, *The Rise and Fall of Ancient Egypt*.

**Accuracy notes:** army sizes are ancient claims and modern estimates (Egypt ~20,000; Hittite chariots 2,500 + 1,000). Date 1274 BC is the commonly used chronology. The UN copy is a replica of the Hittite clay-tablet version.
