# Cannae: The Day Rome Lost Everything
## Full Script — Narrator Only (22 minutes)

**Language:** English  
**Voice:** One narrator (dramatic, witty, fast-paced)  
**Subtitles:** [English text on screen]  
**Characters:** Silent, animate only (expressions, movement, gestures)

---

## 0. COLD OPEN (0:00–0:45)

[*Screen: Black. Wind sound. Fade in: dusty plain. Tens of thousands of tiny stick figures in red formation.*]

**NARRATOR:** 
"August 2nd, 216 BC. Southern Italy. The Roman Republic has just done something it has never done before. It has raised the biggest army in its entire history. Eighty-six thousand men. And across the field? A Carthaginian general, with about fifty thousand... and one working eye."

[*Stick figure with eyepatch stands alone across the battlefield.*]

"By sunset, the Roman army won't be defeated. It won't retreat. It won't surrender. It will simply... cease to exist."

[*TITLE CARD: CANNAE*]

---

## 1. TWO EMPIRES, ONE GRUDGE (0:45–3:30)

[*Map of Mediterranean. Rome in red, Carthage in purple.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"To understand why, you have to know about Rome and Carthage. Two superpowers fighting over the western Mediterranean. One is an empire of farmers and soldiers — stubborn, organized, brutal. The other is a trading empire, wealthy, with the best navy in the world and enough money to hire any mercenary they need."

[*Map animates: a ship sinks in the sea.*]

"They already fought once. Twenty-three years of war. Hundreds of thousands dead. And Rome won."

[*Carthaginian flag falls. Roman flag rises.*]

"But one Carthaginian general didn't handle losing well. His name was Hamilcar Barca."

[*Older Carthaginian man, scarred, angry expression.*]

"And he made a promise to his son at an altar — a promise so strong, it would change history."

[*Young boy (maybe 10 years old) at an altar. Older man's hand on his shoulder.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"That boy was named Hannibal. And Hamilcar told him: 'Never be at peace with Rome.'"

[*Boy's face hardens. Camera zooms in on his eyes.*]

"Hannibal never forgot."

---

## 2. THE SHORTCUT NOBODY TAKES (3:30–5:00)

[*Map: Spain to Alps to Italy, arrow showing the route.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"Years later, Hannibal is a general. War breaks out again. Rome expects to fight in Spain, maybe Africa. Normal places. Hannibal has a different idea."

[*Hannibal on horseback, pointing forward with confidence.*]

"He's going to march overland. Through the Pyrenees. Across southern France. Over the Alps. In autumn. With war elephants."

[*Elephant on a snowy mountain edge, about to fall.*]

"It was insane."

[*Snow, rocks, avalanches. Hannibal's army shrinking as they climb.*]

"He lost half his men on the way. Almost all the elephants died. Soldiers froze. Rockslides buried whole units. But in the end..."

[*Hannibal emerges from fog, at the bottom of the Alps, looking at Italy below.*]

"...a Carthaginian army appeared in northern Italy. Out of nowhere. Rome didn't see it coming."

[*Roman general's face: shock.*]

---

## 3. ROME TRIES. ROME FAILS. TWICE. (5:00–7:00)

[*Fast cuts of battles.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"So Rome sent an army to stop him. At the River Trebia, December 218 BC."

[*Soldiers wading through icy water. Hannibal's soldiers hidden in the fog. Ambush.*]

"Hannibal hid his entire army and waited for the Romans to wade through an icy river before breakfast. By the time they realized they were being attacked, they were already surrounded."

[*Red army getting crushed from both sides.*]

"Thousands drowned trying to escape."

[*Bodies in the water.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"Rome tried again the next year. Lake Trasimene, June 217 BC."

[*Map zooms to lake. Morning fog. Roman army marching along the shore.*]

"Hannibal does the same thing. He hides his army in the hills above a foggy lakeshore. The Romans march straight into the trap. Fifteen thousand Romans die in a few hours. Fifteen thousand more are captured. Their general is killed."

[*Roman general falls, covered in Carthaginian soldiers.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"And somewhere during all this, Hannibal marched through a swamp for days. He got an infection. Lost the sight in one eye. But did he go home? No."

[*Hannibal, sweating, holding his face in pain. Eyepatch appears on his eye.*]

"He just... put on an eyepatch and kept going."

---

## 4. THE MAN WHO SAID 'NO' (7:00–8:45)

[*Rome. Senate. Nervous senators.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"After two catastrophic defeats, Rome did something smart. They gave power to an old senator named Quintus Fabius Maximus. And Fabius had one strategy: don't fight."

[*Fabius, gray-bearded and calm, on a hilltop overlooking Hannibal's army far below.*]

"Instead, he would follow Hannibal around Italy. Cut off his supplies. Pick off small groups. But never engage in a big battle. Hannibal was in enemy territory, far from home, running out of food. Time was on Rome's side."

[*Hannibal's army getting thinner, weaker, hungry.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"It was working. But the Romans absolutely hated it."

[*Roman soldiers complaining, angry faces, gestures.*]

"They gave Fabius a nickname: 'Cunctator.' It means 'The Delayer.' It was not a compliment."

[*Roman senators booing. Fabius ignoring them, still calm.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"So in 216 BC, Rome elects two new consuls. Lucius Aemilius Paullus and Gaius Terentius Varro. And they decide: no more waiting. They raise the biggest army Rome has ever raised, and they're going to find Hannibal and crush him. Today."

[*New generals: one looks wise, one looks overconfident. They stare at each other.*]

"One of them wants to be cautious. The other wants blood."

[*The overconfident one pounds his fist on the table. Papers fly.*]

---

## 5. THE TRAP IS SET (8:45–10:30)

[*Map shows Cannae plain. River Aufidus on one side.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"The two armies meet near a small town called Cannae, in southern Italy. There's a river, the Aufidus, on one side. Open plains everywhere else. Today, the consuls take turns commanding. One day each. Today is Varro's turn — the impatient one."

[*Varro on horseback, confident, stupid grin.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"Varro's strategy is simple. He has twice as many soldiers. He's going to pack them into one massive block — shoulder to shoulder, rank after rank — and push straight through Hannibal's center like a battering ram."

[*Red army arranges into a giant, dense rectangle. Soldiers packed so tight they can barely move.*]

[*Camera view: from above, the Roman formation looks like a solid wall of red.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"Hannibal looks at this and smiles."

[*Hannibal, eyepatch, calm expression. Faint smile.*]

"Because he's already won."

**NARRATOR:**
"He puts his weakest troops — Gauls and Spaniards — in the center. And he makes them form a crescent shape, bulging out toward the Romans."

[*Purple army arranges into a curve, center bulging forward.*]

"Behind them, out of sight, he places his best soldiers: hardened African infantry. On the wings, his cavalry — heavy horsemen on one side, fast Numidian riders on the other."

[*African soldiers hide behind the Gauls. Cavalry spreads out on the flanks.*]

[*Map view: Roman rectangle facing Carthaginian crescent.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"And one more thing. The wind that day is hot and dusty. It's blowing directly into the Romans' faces."

[*Wind animation. Dust clouds blowing toward Roman army. Roman soldiers squinting.*]

---

## 6. THE JAWS CLOSE (10:30–16:00)

[*Battle begins. Drums. Shouting.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"The battle starts. The Roman block charges into Hannibal's center, and at first, it's working. The Gauls and Spaniards are pushed back. Step by step. The crescent flattens. Then it caves inward."

[*Purple crescent bends back. Roman army pushes deeper.*]

[*Close-up: Roman soldiers pushing forward, shields together, determined.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"The Romans are winning. They can feel it. They push deeper and deeper into the gap. More men crowd in behind them."

[*Cannae plain, aerial view: Red army getting tighter and tighter in the middle.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"And then Hannibal says one word: 'Now.'"

[*Hannibal raises his hand. Drops it.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"On both sides, the African soldiers turn inward and slam into the Roman flanks with their spears. At the same time, Hannibal's heavy cavalry have already crushed the Roman horsemen by the river. But they don't stop to celebrate. They don't chase the runners."

[*Cavalry rides in a wide arc, fast, not stopping.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"They ride all the way around, behind the Roman army. They help the Numidians drive off the last Roman cavalry units. Then they charge straight into the Roman rear."

[*Roman army, surrounded on all sides. Purple figures closing in from left, right, and behind.*]

[*Aerial view: Red rectangle now completely surrounded by purple crescent.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"The Roman army is completely surrounded. So tightly packed they can barely raise their swords. The men in the front don't know what's happening. The men in the back can't escape. They're crushed together like sardines in a can."

[*Close-up: Roman soldiers packed together, pushing, shouting, trying to move.*]

[*Sounds: screaming, clashing metal, chaos.*]

[*Sunset. The plain is empty except for bodies. Hannibal rides slowly across the battlefield, looking at what he's done.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"By sunset, it was over. Polybius says around seventy thousand Romans died that day. Other historians say forty-eight thousand. Either way, it was one of the bloodiest single days of battle in all of history."

[*Dead soldiers on the ground. Survivors running. Hannibal's soldiers tending to their wounded.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"The consul Paullus died on the field. Varro escaped — running away on horseback, his overconfidence now a liability. Hannibal lost somewhere between six and eight thousand men."

[*Varro galloping away, face twisted in panic.*]

[*Paullus, surrounded, finally falling.*]

---

## 7. ROME REFUSES TO DIE (16:00–19:30)

[*Carthage. Hannibal's brother Mago stands before the senate, dumping gold rings onto the floor.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"Hannibal's brother sailed home to Carthage with news. He walked into the senate chamber and poured out a pile of gold rings onto the floor. Rings taken from the fingers of dead Roman nobles. Thousands of them."

[*Rings clinking, rolling across the floor.*]

[*Carthaginian senators' faces: shock, then joy.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"Everyone in Rome expected surrender. One of Hannibal's cavalry commanders, Maharbal, begged him to march on Rome immediately."

[*Maharbal on horseback, gesturing toward Rome.*]

"'You know how to win a victory,' Maharbal said. 'But you don't know how to use one.'"

[*Hannibal doesn't move. Stares at the horizon.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"Hannibal hesitated. And that hesitation changed everything. Because Rome... simply refused to give up."

[*Rome. The city is shocked. Terrified. But then... organizing. Building. Recruiting.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"No peace talks. No surrender. They raised new legions. They even armed slaves. And they went back to the strategy everyone had hated: Fabius's strategy. The Delayer's strategy."

[*Fabius, on his hilltop again, sipping wine calmly, vindicated.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"Hannibal stayed in Italy for another thirteen years. He won every battle he fought. But he never took Rome. Never even got close. In the end, Rome took the war to Africa, and Hannibal was finally defeated at Zama in 202 BC."

[*Hannibal, older now, weary, boarding a ship. Sailing away. Rome stands untouched in the distance.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"But Cannae was never forgotten. For more than two thousand years, military academies have studied it. Modern generals still use it as the perfect example of surrounding and destroying a bigger enemy."

[*Military academy. Officers studying maps of Cannae.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"Hannibal won the most important battle of the century. And he still lost the war."

[*Hannibal, alone, looking at the sunset.*]

---

## 8. OUTRO (19:30–22:00)

[*Cannae plain, empty, grass growing over the old battlefield.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"So what do we learn from Cannae? That numbers don't always win. That strategy beats brute force. That one general's genius can matter more than eighty-six thousand soldiers."

[*Hannibal and Varro facing each other one more time, in shadow.*]

**NARRATOR:**
"But also... that even genius has limits. Even Hannibal couldn't break Rome. Because Rome was more than one city or one army. It was an idea. And ideas are harder to kill than men."

[*Rome, burning but unbroken. Soldiers rebuilding.*]

[*Title card: Next episode — Kadesh*]

**NARRATOR:**
"Next time: Kadesh. Where two pharaohs both claimed they won, carved it in stone, and we still don't know who was right. Subscribe to Spears & Tears, so you don't miss it."

[*Subscribe button animates onto screen.*]

[*End credits roll over Hannibal's face, fading to black.*]
