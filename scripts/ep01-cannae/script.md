# Episode 1 — Cannae: How Hannibal Deleted a Roman Army in One Day

**Target length:** ~22 minutes · **Voices:** NARRATOR, SOBIUS, HANNIBAL, VARRO, ELDER (Fabius + Maharbal) · Voice files: `voice/`

**Target length (extended):** 22 minutes, following Hussain Ahn's proven format.

**Main sources:** Polybius, *Histories* III.106–118 · Livy, *History of Rome* XXI–XXII ·
Adrian Goldsworthy, *Cannae* (2001) · Gregory Daly, *Cannae: The Experience of Battle in the Second Punic War* (2002)

Fact-check notes are in `[[ ]]`; visual notes in `( )`. Neither is read aloud.

## 0. Cold open (0:00–0:40)

(Black screen. Wind. Fade in: a dusty plain covered in a sea of tiny red Roman soldiers. Sobius in the front row.)

**NARRATOR:** August 2nd, 216 BC. Southern Italy. The Roman Republic has gathered the biggest army in its entire history. Eight legions. Plus allies. Somewhere around eighty thousand men.

**SOBIUS:** We are SO going to win this.

**NARRATOR:** Across the field stands a Carthaginian general with a much smaller army, a lot of mercenaries who don't speak the same language… and only one working eye.

**HANNIBAL:** Perfect.

**NARRATOR:** By sunset, the Roman army will be gone. Not defeated. Gone.

**SOBIUS:** …Wait, what?

(Title card: SPEARS & TEARS — CANNAE)

[[Polybius: ~80,000 Roman & allied infantry + ~6,000 cavalry. Modern historians often prefer a lower figure, so "somewhere around" is deliberate.]]

## 1. Two cities, one grudge (0:40–2:00)

(Map of the Mediterranean. Rome in red on Italy, Carthage in purple on North Africa.)

**NARRATOR:** To understand Cannae, you need to know about the two superpowers of the western Mediterranean. Rome: farmers, soldiers, and very, very stubborn. And Carthage: a rich trading empire in North Africa, with the best navy around and enough money to hire anyone it needed.

**NARRATOR:** They had already fought one massive war over Sicily. It lasted twenty-three years, and Carthage lost.

**SOBIUS:** Yay!

**NARRATOR:** One of the Carthaginian generals in that war was a man named Hamilcar Barca. And Hamilcar did not handle losing well.

(Young boy Hannibal at an altar, Hamilcar towering behind him.)

**NARRATOR:** According to the story, he took his nine-year-old son to an altar and made him swear that he would never be a friend to Rome.

**HANNIBAL:** I promise, Father.

**NARRATOR:** That boy's name… was Hannibal.

**SOBIUS:** Oh no.

[[First Punic War 264–241 BC. The oath story is in Polybius III.11 and Livy XXI.1; Hannibal was about nine.]]

## 2. The worst shortcut ever (2:00–3:15)

(Map: Spain → Pyrenees → Alps → Italy, a dotted line.)

**NARRATOR:** Years later, Hannibal is grown up, in command of Carthage's army in Spain, and ready to keep his promise. In 218 BC, war breaks out again. Rome expects to fight in Spain, or in Africa. Normal places.

**NARRATOR:** Hannibal has a different idea. He will march overland… through the Pyrenees… across southern France… and over the Alps. In the autumn. With war elephants.

**SOBIUS:** Elephants? In the MOUNTAINS?

(Elephant sliding down a snowy slope, trumpeting.)

**NARRATOR:** It was brutal. Snow, landslides, and local tribes rolling rocks onto his men. Hannibal lost a huge part of his army on the way. But in the end, he did it. A Carthaginian army had appeared in northern Italy, out of nowhere.

**SOBIUS:** That's… that's cheating!

**HANNIBAL:** It's called planning.

[[Crossing of the Alps, autumn 218 BC. Polybius says he reached Italy with ~20,000 infantry and ~6,000 cavalry, far fewer than he set out with. Most of the elephants died soon afterwards.]]

## 3. Rome loses. Then loses again. (3:15–4:40)

(Quick montage, each with a "ROMAN LOSS" stamp.)

**NARRATOR:** Rome sends an army to deal with him. At the River Trebia, in December, Hannibal tricks the Romans into wading through an icy river before breakfast, then ambushes them.

**SOBIUS:** (teeth chattering) S-s-so c-cold…

**NARRATOR:** The next year Rome tries again. At Lake Trasimene, Hannibal hides his entire army in the hills above a foggy lakeshore… and waits. The Roman army marches straight into the trap. The Roman commander, the consul Flaminius, is killed.

(Hannibal in a swamp, one eye red and swollen, then the eyepatch appears.)

**NARRATOR:** Oh, and somewhere along the way, Hannibal spent days marching through flooded marshes, caught an infection, and lost the sight in one eye. It did not slow him down at all.

**HANNIBAL:** I only need one eye to watch Romans run.

[[Trebia: Dec 218 BC. Trasimene: June 217 BC. Eye lost in the Arno marshes, spring 217 BC (Polybius III.79, Livy XXII.2).]]

## 4. The man who refused to fight (4:40–5:50)

(An old Roman with a long grey beard, sitting calmly on a hill, watching Hannibal from far away.)

**NARRATOR:** After two disasters, Rome tries something new. They give power to an old senator named Quintus Fabius Maximus. And Fabius has a very clever plan.

**ELDER:** We will not fight him.

**SOBIUS:** …That's the plan?

**NARRATOR:** Fabius follows Hannibal around Italy, cuts his supplies, picks off small groups… but refuses to fight a big battle. Hannibal is in enemy land, far from home. Time is on Rome's side.

**NARRATOR:** It was smart. And the Romans absolutely hated it. They gave Fabius a nickname: Cunctator. "The Delayer." It was not a compliment.

**NARRATOR:** So for 216 BC, Rome elects two new consuls, Lucius Aemilius Paullus and Gaius Terentius Varro, and gives them the biggest army it has ever raised. The goal: find Hannibal, and crush him.

**VARRO:** No more waiting! No more delaying! We attack!

**SOBIUS:** Yeah! …Wait, why am I in the front row?

[[Fabius was appointed dictator in 217 BC. Livy portrays Varro as the reckless one and Paullus as the cautious one; modern historians think this is partly Roman blame-shifting. Mention this in the description.]]

## 5. The trap is set (5:50–7:20)

(Top-down cartoon map: the plain of Cannae, the River Aufidus on one side.)

**NARRATOR:** The two armies meet near a small town called Cannae, in southern Italy, beside the River Aufidus. The two consuls take turns commanding, one day each. On August 2nd, it's Varro's turn.

**VARRO:** Today is my day.

**NARRATOR:** Varro's plan is simple. He packs his infantry into a deep, heavy block in the middle, and plans to smash straight through the Carthaginian center like a battering ram.

(Red block slowly pushing forward. Sobius squished in the middle of it.)

**SOBIUS:** I can't… move… my arms…

**NARRATOR:** Hannibal looks at this, and does something very strange. He puts his weakest troops, Gauls and Spaniards, in the center, and pushes them forward into a bulge, curving out towards the Romans.

**NARRATOR:** Behind them, on both sides, out of sight, he places his best soldiers: veteran African infantry. And on the wings, his cavalry. Heavy horsemen on one side, fast Numidian riders on the other.

**HANNIBAL:** Let them come.

**NARRATOR:** One more thing. Ancient writers say the wind that day blew hot and dusty… straight into the Romans' faces.

**SOBIUS:** (coughing) Why is it always ME?

[[Formation: Polybius III.113–114. Romans had roughly twice the infantry; Hannibal had more and better cavalry (~10,000). The dusty wind, the Volturnus, is from Livy XXII.46.]]

## 6. The jaws close (7:20–9:00)

(Animated map, then character shots.)

**NARRATOR:** The battle begins. The giant Roman block charges into Hannibal's center, and at first, it works. The Gauls and Spaniards are pushed back. Step by step. The bulge flattens… then caves inward.

**SOBIUS:** They're running! We're winning!

**NARRATOR:** The Romans push deeper and deeper into the gap. More and more of them crowd into the middle.

**HANNIBAL:** (calmly) Now.

**NARRATOR:** On both sides, the African veterans turn inward and slam into the Roman flanks. Meanwhile, Hannibal's heavy cavalry has already crushed the Roman horsemen by the river. Instead of chasing them, they ride all the way around, behind the Roman army… help the Numidians drive off the allied cavalry… and then charge into the Roman rear.

(The purple shapes close around the red block like a pair of jaws. Sobius looks left, right, behind.)

**SOBIUS:** Uh… guys? Why are there Carthaginians… behind us?

**NARRATOR:** The Roman army is completely surrounded. So tightly packed, most soldiers can't even raise their swords. The men in the middle can't see what's happening, and they can't escape.

(Cut to black. Only sounds. Then a quiet, dusty field at sunset. Sobius alone, holding a broken spear, crying.)

**NARRATOR:** By the end of the day, the Roman army had been destroyed. Polybius says around seventy thousand were killed. Livy gives a smaller number, about forty-eight thousand. Either way, it was one of the bloodiest single days of battle in all of history.

**NARRATOR:** The consul Paullus died on the field. Varro escaped. Hannibal lost somewhere between six and eight thousand men.

[[Polybius III.117: ~70,000 dead. Livy XXII.49: 45,500 infantry + 2,700 cavalry. Hannibal's losses: Polybius ~5,700, Livy ~8,000.]]

## 7. Rome refuses to quit (9:00–10:20)

(Carthage senate hall. A messenger pours out a huge pile of gold rings.)

**NARRATOR:** Hannibal's brother sailed home to Carthage with the news. According to Livy, he poured a pile of gold rings onto the floor of the senate. Rings taken from dead Roman nobles.

**SOBIUS:** That's… so rude.

**NARRATOR:** Everyone expected Rome to surrender. One of Hannibal's cavalry commanders, Maharbal, begged him to march on Rome right away.

**ELDER:** You know how to win a victory, Hannibal. But you do not know how to use one.

**HANNIBAL:** …I'll think about it.

**NARRATOR:** Hannibal didn't march on Rome. And Rome… simply refused to give up. No peace talks. They raised new legions. They even armed slaves. And they went back to the strategy everyone had hated: the Delayer's strategy.

(Fabius on his hill, sipping from a cup.)

**ELDER:** Told you.

**NARRATOR:** Hannibal stayed in Italy for another thirteen years. He was never defeated there. But he never took Rome, either. In the end, Rome took the war to Africa, and in 202 BC, Hannibal was finally beaten at the Battle of Zama.

[[Gold rings: Livy XXIII.12 (Mago). Maharbal's line: Livy XXII.51. Hannibal left Italy in 203 BC; Zama 202 BC.]]

## 8. Legacy & outro (10:20–11:00)

**NARRATOR:** But Cannae was never forgotten. For more than two thousand years, generals have studied it as the perfect example of surrounding and destroying a bigger army. Hannibal won the battle of the century… and still lost the war.

**SOBIUS:** So… we lost the battle… but we won the war?

**NARRATOR:** Yes.

**SOBIUS:** So I can stop crying?

**NARRATOR:** Next episode: the Battle of Kadesh, where two kings both claimed they won… and carved it in stone.

**SOBIUS:** (sobbing again) More wars?!

**NARRATOR:** Subscribe to Spears & Tears, so you don't miss it.
