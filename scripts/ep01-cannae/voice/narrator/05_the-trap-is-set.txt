The two armies meet near a small town called Cannae, in southern Italy. There's a river, the Aufidus, on one side. Open plains everywhere else. Today, the consuls take turns commanding. One day each. Today is Varro's turn — the impatient one.

Varro's strategy is simple. He has twice as many soldiers. He's going to pack them into one massive block — shoulder to shoulder, rank after rank — and push straight through Hannibal's center like a battering ram.

Hannibal looks at this and smiles.

Because he's already won.

He puts his weakest troops — Gauls and Spaniards — in the center. And he makes them form a crescent shape, bulging out toward the Romans.

Behind them, out of sight, he places his best soldiers: hardened African infantry. On the wings, his cavalry — heavy horsemen on one side, fast Numidian riders on the other.

And one more thing. The wind that day is hot and dusty. It's blowing directly into the Romans' faces.
