Hannibal's brother sailed home to Carthage with news. He walked into the senate chamber and poured out a pile of gold rings onto the floor. Rings taken from the fingers of dead Roman nobles. Thousands of them.

Everyone in Rome expected surrender. One of Hannibal's cavalry commanders, Maharbal, begged him to march on Rome immediately.

'You know how to win a victory,' Maharbal said. 'But you don't know how to use one.'

Hannibal hesitated. And that hesitation changed everything. Because Rome... simply refused to give up.

No peace talks. No surrender. They raised new legions. They even armed slaves. And they went back to the strategy everyone had hated: Fabius's strategy. The Delayer's strategy.

Hannibal stayed in Italy for another thirteen years. He won every battle he fought. But he never took Rome. Never even got close. In the end, Rome took the war to Africa, and Hannibal was finally defeated at Zama in 202 BC.

But Cannae was never forgotten. For more than two thousand years, military academies have studied it. Modern generals still use it as the perfect example of surrounding and destroying a bigger enemy.

Hannibal won the most important battle of the century. And he still lost the war.
