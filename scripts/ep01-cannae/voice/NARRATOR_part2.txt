The two armies meet near a small town called Cannae, in southern Italy, beside the River Aufidus. The two consuls take turns commanding, one day each. On August 2nd, it's Varro's turn. <break time="2.0s" /> Varro's plan is simple. He packs his infantry into a deep, heavy block in the middle, and plans to smash straight through the Carthaginian center like a battering ram. <break time="2.0s" /> Hannibal looks at this, and does something very strange. He puts his weakest troops, Gauls and Spaniards, in the center, and pushes them forward into a bulge, curving out towards the Romans. <break time="2.0s" /> Behind them, on both sides, out of sight, he places his best soldiers: veteran African infantry. And on the wings, his cavalry. Heavy horsemen on one side, fast Numidian riders on the other. <break time="2.0s" /> One more thing. Ancient writers say the wind that day blew hot and dusty… straight into the Romans' faces. <break time="2.0s" /> The battle begins. The giant Roman block charges into Hannibal's center, and at first, it works. The Gauls and Spaniards are pushed back. Step by step. The bulge flattens… then caves inward. <break time="2.0s" /> The Romans push deeper and deeper into the gap. More and more of them crowd into the middle. <break time="2.0s" /> On both sides, the African veterans turn inward and slam into the Roman flanks. Meanwhile, Hannibal's heavy cavalry has already crushed the Roman horsemen by the river. Instead of chasing them, they ride all the way around, behind the Roman army… help the Numidians drive off the allied cavalry… and then charge into the Roman rear. <break time="2.0s" /> The Roman army is completely surrounded. So tightly packed, most soldiers can't even raise their swords. The men in the middle can't see what's happening, and they can't escape. <break time="2.0s" /> By the end of the day, the Roman army had been destroyed. Polybius says around seventy thousand were killed. Livy gives a smaller number, about forty-eight thousand. Either way, it was one of the bloodiest single days of battle in all of history. <break time="2.0s" /> The consul Paullus died on the field. Varro escaped. Hannibal lost somewhere between six and eight thousand men. <break time="2.0s" /> Hannibal's brother sailed home to Carthage with the news. According to Livy, he poured a pile of gold rings onto the floor of the senate. Rings taken from dead Roman nobles. <break time="2.0s" /> Everyone expected Rome to surrender. One of Hannibal's cavalry commanders, Maharbal, begged him to march on Rome right away. <break time="2.0s" /> Hannibal didn't march on Rome. And Rome… simply refused to give up. No peace talks. They raised new legions. They even armed slaves. And they went back to the strategy everyone had hated: the Delayer's strategy. <break time="2.0s" /> Hannibal stayed in Italy for another thirteen years. He was never defeated there. But he never took Rome, either. In the end, Rome took the war to Africa, and in 202 BC, Hannibal was finally beaten at the Battle of Zama. <break time="2.0s" /> But Cannae was never forgotten. For more than two thousand years, generals have studied it as the perfect example of surrounding and destroying a bigger army.
