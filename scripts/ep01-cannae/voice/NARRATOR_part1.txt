August 2nd, 216 BC. Southern Italy. The Roman Republic has gathered the biggest army in its entire history. Eight legions. Plus allies. Somewhere around eighty thousand men. <break time="2.0s" /> Across the field stands a Carthaginian general with a much smaller army, a lot of mercenaries who don't speak the same language… and only one working eye. <break time="2.0s" /> By sunset, the Roman army will be gone. Not defeated. Gone. <break time="2.0s" /> To understand Cannae, you need to know about the two superpowers of the western Mediterranean. Rome: farmers, soldiers, and very, very stubborn. And Carthage: a rich trading empire in North Africa, with the best navy around and enough money to hire anyone it needed. <break time="2.0s" /> They had already fought one massive war over Sicily. It lasted twenty-three years, and Carthage lost. <break time="2.0s" /> One of the Carthaginian generals in that war was a man named Hamilcar Barca. And Hamilcar did not handle losing well. <break time="2.0s" /> According to the story, he took his nine-year-old son to an altar and made him swear that he would never be a friend to Rome. <break time="2.0s" /> That boy's name… was Hannibal. <break time="2.0s" /> Years later, Hannibal is grown up, in command of Carthage's army in Spain, and ready to keep his promise. In 218 BC, war breaks out again. Rome expects to fight in Spain, or in Africa. Normal places. <break time="2.0s" /> Hannibal has a different idea. He will march overland… through the Pyrenees… across southern France… and over the Alps. In the autumn. With war elephants. <break time="2.0s" /> It was brutal. Snow, landslides, and local tribes rolling rocks onto his men. Hannibal lost a huge part of his army on the way. But in the end, he did it. A Carthaginian army had appeared in northern Italy, out of nowhere. <break time="2.0s" /> Rome sends an army to deal with him. At the River Trebia, in December, Hannibal tricks the Romans into wading through an icy river before breakfast, then ambushes them. <break time="2.0s" /> The next year Rome tries again. At Lake Trasimene, Hannibal hides his entire army in the hills above a foggy lakeshore… and waits. The Roman army marches straight into the trap. The Roman commander, the consul Flaminius, is killed. <break time="2.0s" /> Oh, and somewhere along the way, Hannibal spent days marching through flooded marshes, caught an infection, and lost the sight in one eye. It did not slow him down at all. <break time="2.0s" /> After two disasters, Rome tries something new. They give power to an old senator named Quintus Fabius Maximus. And Fabius has a very clever plan. <break time="2.0s" /> Fabius follows Hannibal around Italy, cuts his supplies, picks off small groups… but refuses to fight a big battle. Hannibal is in enemy land, far from home. Time is on Rome's side. <break time="2.0s" /> It was smart. And the Romans absolutely hated it. They gave Fabius a nickname: Cunctator. "The Delayer." It was not a compliment. <break time="2.0s" /> So for 216 BC, Rome elects two new consuls, Lucius Aemilius Paullus and Gaius Terentius Varro, and gives them the biggest army it has ever raised. The goal: find Hannibal, and crush him.
